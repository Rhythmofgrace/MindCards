UNIT 1: FUNDAMENTALS OF TIME SERIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Topic: Introduction to Time Series]

Q: 1. What is a Time Series and why is it important in Computer Science and Business?
A:
A time series is a sequential set of data observations recorded at successive, uniform intervals of time (e.g., hourly, daily, monthly).
- Computer Science: Monitors server workload, CPU/memory usage, network bandwidth traffic, and detects cybersecurity anomalies.
- Business & Finance: Tracks stock market fluctuations, sales forecasts, inventory demands, and economic indicators over time.
- Core Purpose: Analyzes past patterns to understand underlying dynamics and forecast future behavior.

Q: 2. State the primary objectives and key applications of Time Series Analysis.
A:
Time series analysis serves four principal objectives across engineering and commerce:
- Description: Identifying and isolating structural patterns such as trends, cycles, and seasonal variations.
- Explanation: Understanding how correlated external factors influence variable behavior over time.
- Forecasting: Predicting future values to optimize resource scheduling, capacity planning, and budgets.
- Control: Setting threshold alerts in automated systems (e.g., automated server scaling upon traffic surge).

Q: 3. What is the difference between Continuous and Discrete Time Series?
A:
Time series data is classified by how observations are sampled along the time continuum:
- Continuous Time Series: Observations are recorded continuously over every infinitesimal instant of time (e.g., analog seismograph signals, continuous ECG monitors, barometric pressure charts).
- Discrete Time Series: Observations are sampled and recorded at fixed, discrete temporal points (e.g., daily stock closing price, monthly website page views, annual GDP).

Q: 4. Distinguish between Cross-Sectional, Time Series, and Panel Data.
A:
Data structures differ based on temporal depth and cross-sectional entity breadth:
- Cross-Sectional Data: Observations collected across multiple subjects (e.g., 500 companies) at a single fixed point in time.
- Time Series Data: Observations collected for a single subject across multiple successive time intervals.
- Panel (Longitudinal) Data: Hybrid structure combining both, tracking multiple subjects repeatedly across multiple successive time intervals.

[Topic: Components of Time Series (Four Forces)]

Q: 1. What are the "Four Forces" (Components) of a Time Series?
A:
A classical time series is governed by four distinct underlying behavioral components:
- Secular Trend (T): Long-term upward, downward, or steady movement over extended periods.
- Seasonal Variations (S): Short-term, regular periodic fluctuations repeating within a span of one year.
- Cyclical Variations (C): Medium-to-long term wave-like oscillations around the trend associated with business cycles.
- Irregular / Random Fluctuations (I): Unpredictable, erratic shocks caused by random exogenous events.

Q: 2. Define Secular Trend (T) and describe its long-term characteristics.
A:
Secular trend represents the smooth, underlying long-term direction of a series over decades or years:
- Sustained Direction: Exhibits a consistent upward movement (e.g., global population, e-commerce adoption) or downward movement (e.g., death rates from eradicated diseases).
- Isolates Noise: Ignores short-term seasonal blips and daily market volatility.
- Forms Baseline: Serves as the foundational baseline around which other short-term forces oscillate.

Q: 3. Explain Seasonal Variations (S) and contrast them with Cyclical Variations (C).
A:
Seasonal and cyclical components differ in their periodicity and underlying drivers:
- Seasonal Variations (S): Strict periodic rhythm with cycles completing within ≤ 1 year (e.g., retail surge during festivals, ice cream sales in summer); driven by climate or institutional conventions.
- Cyclical Variations (C): Long-term non-periodic wave oscillations spanning 3 to 10+ years; driven by economic business cycles (Prosperity, Recession, Depression, Recovery).

Q: 4. What are Irregular or Random Fluctuations (I) and what causes them?
A:
Irregular fluctuations represent unpredictable, random residual variations left after accounting for T, S, and C:
- Unforeseen Shocks: Triggered by unpredictable events such as natural disasters (earthquakes, floods), wars, sudden strikes, or pandemics.
- Non-Mathematical: Cannot be predicted mathematically or modeled deterministically.
- Short-Lived: Causes sharp temporary disruptions that gradually subside as systems return to baseline equilibrium.

[Topic: Mathematical Decomposition Models]

Q: 1. Explain the Additive Decomposition Model (Y = T + S + C + I).
A:
The Additive Model assumes the observed series value is the linear sum of all four independent components:
- Equation: Y_t = T_t + S_t + C_t + I_t.
- Measurement Unit: All components (T, S, C, I) are expressed in identical original units of measurement as Y.
- Core Assumption: Seasonal swings maintain a constant absolute magnitude regardless of whether the trend level rises or falls.

Q: 2. Explain the Multiplicative Decomposition Model (Y = T * S * C * I).
A:
The Multiplicative Model assumes components interact proportionally rather than operating independently:
- Equation: Y_t = T_t * S_t * C_t * I_t.
- Measurement Unit: Trend (T) is in original physical units; Seasonal (S), Cyclical (C), and Irregular (I) are dimensionless indices or percentage ratios.
- Core Assumption: The magnitude of seasonal fluctuations is directly proportional to the overall level of the secular trend.

Q: 3. Compare the merits and demerits of the Additive Model.
A:
The additive framework offers simplicity but carries operational limitations:
- Merits: Highly intuitive, easy to compute by direct subtraction, and simple to interpret when seasonal swings are uniform.
- Demerits: Unrealistic when economic growth inflates seasonal variances over time; does not capture compounding percentage growth rates.

Q: 4. When should you choose the Multiplicative Model over the Additive Model?
A:
Model selection depends on the visual relationship between seasonal amplitude and trend level:
- Choose Multiplicative: When seasonal peaks and troughs grow wider as the overall trend increases (most economic, sales, and web traffic series).
- Choose Additive: When seasonal oscillation amplitude remains virtually constant over time regardless of trend elevation.
- Transformation: A multiplicative model can be converted into an additive model by taking logarithms: log(Y) = log(T) + log(S) + log(C) + log(I).

[Topic: Non-Mathematical Trend Estimation]

Q: 1. What is the Free-Hand Curve Method and what are its primary limitations?
A:
The Free-Hand Curve method plots original data on a Cartesian graph and manually sketches a smooth trend line:
- Procedure: Visualizes raw points and manually draws an overarching smooth curve through the center of fluctuations.
- Advantages: Extremely simple, fast, and does not require complex statistical computations.
- Major Limitations: Highly subjective and arbitrary; different analysts draw different lines, making objective mathematical forecasting impossible.

Q: 2. Explain the Method of Semi-Averages for determining linear trend.
A:
The Method of Semi-Averages divides the entire time series dataset into two equal halves:
- Step 1: Split data into two equal temporal parts (e.g., two 4-year periods for an 8-year dataset).
- Step 2: Compute the arithmetic mean (semi-average) of values in each half.
- Step 3: Plot each semi-average against the mid-point of its respective time sub-period.
- Step 4: Connect the two plotted points with a straight line to establish the secular linear trend.

Q: 3. How do you apply the Method of Semi-Averages when the number of periods is odd?
A:
When total time periods N is odd, data cannot be divided into two equal integers:
- Omission Method: The central middle year/period is completely omitted from calculations.
- Division: The remaining (N - 1) years are then split into two perfectly equal halves.
- Example: In an 11-year series (2010–2020), omit the 6th year (2015); calculate the semi-average for 2010–2014 and for 2016–2020.

Q: 4. State the merits and demerits of the Method of Semi-Averages.
A:
The Semi-Average technique balances simplicity with objectivity:
- Merits: Completely objective (yields identical results regardless of who computes it); simple arithmetic without calculus.
- Demerits: Strictly presumes a linear trend line; completely inappropriate if underlying trend is curvilinear, exponential, or parabolic.

[Topic: Mathematical Trend Fitting (Least Squares)]

Q: 1. Explain the Principle of Least Squares for fitting a Linear Trend line (y = a + bx).
A:
The Method of Least Squares minimizes the sum of squared vertical residuals between observed data and trend values:
- Trend Equation: y = a + bx, where 'a' is the y-intercept at x = 0, and 'b' is the constant rate of growth (slope).
- Normal Equations: 
  1) Σy = Na + bΣx
  2) Σxy = aΣx + bΣx²
- Objective: Ensures that Σ(y - y_hat) = 0 and Σ(y - y_hat)² is minimum.

Q: 2. How are Least Squares normal equations simplified by shifting the origin (Σx = 0)?
A:
By setting origin x = 0 at the temporal center of the dataset, Σx evaluates to zero:
- Intercept Formula: a = Σy / N (arithmetic mean of y).
- Slope Formula: b = Σxy / Σx².
- Even Periods: When N is even, scale deviations in half-year units: x = ..., -3, -1, +1, +3, ... so Σx = 0 remains valid.
- Advantage: Decouples normal equations, eliminating simultaneous matrix elimination.

Q: 3. Explain the procedure for fitting a Second-Degree Parabolic Curve (y = a + bx + cx²).
A:
A parabolic quadratic curve models non-linear trends exhibiting acceleration or turning points:
- Equation: y = a + bx + cx².
- Normal Equations (when origin is shifted such that Σx = 0):
  1) Σy = Na + cΣx²
  2) Σxy = bΣx²
  3) Σx²y = aΣx² + cΣx⁴
- Parameter 'b' is determined directly as b = Σxy / Σx²; parameters 'a' and 'c' are solved simultaneously from equations 1 and 3.

Q: 4. Explain the formulation and fitting of an Exponential Trend Curve (y = ab^x).
A:
An exponential trend models geometric compounding growth common in IT adoption and population:
- Equation: y = a * b^x.
- Log-Linearization: Taking logarithms on both sides converts the exponential into a linear equation: log(y) = log(a) + x * log(b).
- Let Y = log(y), A = log(a), and B = log(b); the linear equation becomes Y = A + Bx.
- Solved via standard least squares equations for A and B; then a = antilog(A) and b = antilog(B).

[Topic: Moving Average Method]

Q: 1. What is the Moving Average Method and how does it smooth out fluctuations?
A:
The Moving Average method computes successive overlapping arithmetic means over a fixed time window 'm':
- Smoothing Action: Averages out short-term seasonal and irregular oscillations, highlighting the underlying trend.
- Window Selection: Setting window 'm' equal to the period of cyclical or seasonal oscillations completely neutralizes that cycle.
- Non-Parametric: Does not assume any rigid mathematical function (linear, quadratic), adapting dynamically to local curvature.

Q: 2. How do you calculate Odd-Period Moving Averages (3-yearly and 5-yearly)?
A:
For an odd period 'm', the calculated average directly aligns with the exact central year:
- 3-Yearly Moving Average: Average of y1, y2, y3 is placed against year 2; average of y2, y3, y4 placed against year 3.
- 5-Yearly Moving Average: Average of y1 to y5 placed against year 3; average of y2 to y6 placed against year 4.
- Boundary Loss: A 3-yearly moving average loses 1 year at each end; a 5-yearly moving average loses 2 years at each end.

Q: 3. Why is Centering required for Even-Period Moving Averages (4-yearly centered)?
A:
When period 'm' is even, the arithmetic mean falls between two calendar years:
- Step 1: Calculate 4-yearly moving totals and 4-yearly moving averages; they sit halfway between years (e.g., between years 2 and 3).
- Step 2: Average two consecutive 4-yearly moving averages together; this 'centered' value now aligns directly with an exact calendar year (year 3).
- Eliminates phase distortion and temporal misalignment with original observations.

Q: 4. State the key merits and demerits of the Moving Average Method.
A:
Moving averages provide robust local smoothing with specific operational trade-offs:
- Merits: Highly flexible, simple to compute, effectively dampens periodic cycles when cycle length is known.
- Demerits: Loses data points at the beginning and end of the series; cannot provide mathematical forecast values for future unobserved periods.

[Topic: Measurement of Seasonal Indices]

Q: 1. What is a Seasonal Index and what does it measure?
A:
A Seasonal Index is a normalized percentage ratio measuring the relative seasonal intensity for a given sub-period:
- Baseline: The annual average across all seasons is normalized to 100%.
- Interpretation: An index of 130% for Q4 indicates sales in Q4 are typically 30% above the average quarterly level.
- Balance: For quarterly data, the sum of four seasonal indices must equal 400 (or mean = 100); for monthly data, the sum must equal 1200.

Q: 2. Explain the Method of Simple Averages for calculating seasonal indices.
A:
The simplest technique for isolating seasonal variations from quarterly or monthly data:
- Step 1: Arrange data in a two-dimensional grid with years in rows and seasons (quarters/months) in columns.
- Step 2: Compute the average value for each column across all years (Quarterly Averages x_bar).
- Step 3: Compute the Grand Average G_bar of all quarterly averages.
- Step 4: Seasonal Index for Quarter i = (x_bar_i / G_bar) * 100 (adjusted so sum = 400).
- Assumption: Presumes the secular trend is flat; biased if strong long-term trend exists.

Q: 3. Explain the Ratio-to-Trend Method for isolating seasonal variations.
A:
Ratio-to-Trend accounts for an underlying secular trend before extracting seasonal indices:
- Step 1: Fit a mathematical trend line (e.g., least squares line y = a + bx) and compute trend values T for each period.
- Step 2: Divide each actual observation Y by its corresponding trend value T: (Y / T) * 100.
- Step 3: Group ratios by season across years and eliminate irregular outliers by taking medians or trimmed means.
- Step 4: Adjust final seasonal indices so their sum equals 400 (quarterly) or 1200 (monthly).

Q: 4. Explain the Ratio-to-Moving Average Method and why it is considered the most reliable.
A:
Ratio-to-Moving Average isolates seasonal indices by eliminating both trend and cyclical components:
- Step 1: Compute a 12-month (or 4-quarter centered) moving average; this captures combined Trend and Cyclical forces (T * C).
- Step 2: Divide original observations Y by centered moving averages: Y / (T * C) = (T * S * C * I) / (T * C) = S * I.
- Step 3: Average the resulting (S * I) ratios for each season across years to smooth out random noise (I), leaving pure Seasonal Index (S).
- Step 4: Normalize indices to 100% baseline; widely considered the gold standard in decomposition.

[Topic: Deseasonalization and Link Relatives Method]

Q: 1. What is Deseasonalization and how is a time series seasonally adjusted?
A:
Deseasonalization removes regular seasonal swings from raw data to expose underlying trend and cyclical movements:
- Additive Adjustment: Deseasonalized Series = Y - S.
- Multiplicative Adjustment: Deseasonalized Series = (Y / S) * 100.
- Purpose: Enables fair comparison of performance across consecutive periods (e.g., comparing Q4 holiday sales against Q1 baseline without seasonal bias).

Q: 2. Explain Pearson's Method of Link Relatives for computing seasonal indices.
A:
The Method of Link Relatives expresses each period's value as a percentage of the immediately preceding period:
- Step 1: Calculate Link Relative = (Current Period Value / Preceding Period Value) * 100.
- Step 2: Average link relatives for each season across all years (using median or mean).
- Step 3: Convert link relatives into Chain Relatives with the first period fixed at 100: Chain Relative = (Current Link Relative * Preceding Chain Relative) / 100.
- Step 4: Correct for trend discrepancy between initial and final chain relative, then normalize.

Q: 3. Compare the four methods of measuring seasonal variations.
A:
Each method of measuring seasonal variations balances simplicity with statistical rigor:
- Simple Averages: Easiest, but valid only when trend is absent or negligible.
- Ratio-to-Trend: Effective when trend follows an exact known mathematical curve (linear/quadratic).
- Ratio-to-Moving Average: Most robust and flexible; eliminates unknown trends without assuming strict linearity.
- Link Relatives: Comprehensive, but computationally intensive and sensitive to extreme boundary values.

Q: 4. How do businesses and economists interpret seasonally adjusted time series?
A:
Seasonally adjusted figures provide clear insight into true business momentum:
- Real Growth vs. Calendar Effect: Reveals whether a sales spike represents actual business expansion or merely an expected annual holiday pattern.
- Policy Decisions: Central banks and government agencies use seasonally adjusted unemployment and inflation data to formulate economic policies.
- Forecasting: Future baseline projections are obtained by forecasting deseasonalized trend, then multiplying by seasonal index.

[Topic: Growth Curves in IT and Technology Adoption]

Q: 1. What are Growth Curves and why are they used to model IT and Technology Adoption?
A:
Growth curves are mathematical models depicting the non-linear trajectory of growth towards a saturation ceiling:
- Asymptotic Ceiling: Unlike linear trends, technology products cannot grow indefinitely; they are constrained by total market capacity (saturation).
- IT Lifecycle: Models internet user adoption, smartphone penetration, cloud migration, and software user registration over time.
- Stages: Characterized by an initial slow incubation phase, an exponential acceleration phase, and eventual deceleration towards maturity.

Q: 2. Explain the Modified Exponential Growth Curve (y = k + ab^x).
A:
The Modified Exponential curve models growth where the rate of growth declines exponentially towards an asymptote:
- Equation: y = k + a * b^x, where k is the upper asymptotic ceiling (k > 0), a is negative, and 0 < b < 1.
- Property: The difference between the ceiling k and actual value y decreases by a constant percentage ratio 'b' each period.
- Application: Models adoption of technologies that experience immediate early takeoff followed by steady deceleration.

Q: 3. Explain the Gompertz Growth Curve (y = k * a^(b^x)) and its applications in IT.
A:
The Gompertz curve models asymmetrical S-shaped growth where percentage growth declines at a constant rate:
- Equation: y = k * a^(b^x), where k is the saturation limit, 0 < a < 1, and 0 < b < 1.
- Asymmetry: Inflection point occurs early (at y = k / e ≈ 37% of saturation ceiling), meaning growth slows down sooner than in logistic models.
- IT Application: Widely used to predict new software licensing adoption, mobile subscriber base, and organizational cloud transformation.

Q: 4. Explain the Logistic Growth Curve (S-Curve) and its three adoption phases.
A:
The Logistic Curve produces a perfectly symmetrical S-shaped diffusion curve widely used in tech adoption:
- Equation: y = k / (1 + b * e^(-ax)), where k is the carrying capacity / market saturation limit.
- Symmetry: Point of inflection occurs precisely at the midpoint y = k / 2, where maximum growth velocity is attained.
- Three Phases: 1) Slow initial adoption (Innovators/Early Adopters), 2) Rapid exponential takeoff (Early/Late Majority), 3) Saturation plateau (Laggards).

[Topic: Smoothing Techniques & Exponential Smoothing]

Q: 1. What is Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES) and why is it preferred over moving averages?
A:
SES is a weighted averaging technique that assigns exponentially declining weights to progressively older observations:
- Recursive Formula: F_(t+1) = α * Y_t + (1 - α) * F_t, where α is the smoothing constant (0 ≤ α ≤ 1).
- Recency Weighting: Recent data receives the highest weight; older data decays exponentially: α(1 - α)^k.
- Memory Efficiency: Requires storing only the latest observation Y_t and current forecast F_t rather than a long historical window.

Q: 2. How does the smoothing parameter α influence forecast responsiveness vs. stability?
A:
The choice of smoothing constant α governs the trade-off between speed and noise filtering:
- High α (close to 1): Gives heavy weight to the most recent observation; highly responsive to sudden shifts, but vulnerable to noisy spikes.
- Low α (close to 0): Gives heavy weight to historical smoothed forecast; produces a very smooth, stable baseline, but lags behind genuine structural shifts.
- Typical Values: Commonly calibrated between 0.1 and 0.3 via error minimization (MSE).

Q: 3. Explain Holt's Linear Exponential Smoothing method and how it handles trends.
A:
Holt's two-parameter exponential smoothing extends SES to handle series containing secular linear trends:
- Level Equation: L_t = α * Y_t + (1 - α) * (L_(t-1) + T_(t-1)), smoothing the baseline level.
- Trend Equation: T_t = β * (L_t - L_(t-1)) + (1 - β) * T_(t-1), updating the trend slope using parameter β (0 ≤ β ≤ 1).
- Forecast Formula: Forecast for h steps ahead is F_(t+h) = L_t + h * T_t.

Q: 4. What is Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing?
A:
Holt-Winters is a three-parameter smoothing model capturing level, trend, and seasonal patterns simultaneously:
- Three Smoothing Parameters: α for level, β for trend slope, and γ for seasonal indices.
- Multiplicative Holt-Winters: F_(t+h) = (L_t + h * T_t) * S_(t+h-m), standard for economic and business forecasting.
- Capable of generating accurate multi-step seasonal forecasts with low computational overhead.

[Topic: Stationarity and White Noise]

Q: 1. What is a Stationary Time Series and why is stationarity a prerequisite for modeling?
A:
A stationary time series is one whose statistical properties do not depend on the specific point in time at which it is observed:
- Model Stability: Forecasting algorithms (like ARIMA) assume underlying generative parameters remain constant across time.
- Reliable Inference: Without stationarity, statistical metrics (mean, variance, auto-correlation) vary over time, rendering past observations useless for predicting the future.

Q: 2. Define Weak (Covariance) Stationarity in terms of Mean, Variance, and Autocovariance.
A:
A series Y_t is weakly (second-order) stationary if it satisfies three strict criteria:
- Constant Mean: E[Y_t] = μ for all time t (no secular trend).
- Constant Variance: Var(Y_t) = σ² for all time t (homoscedasticity, no volatility explosions).
- Time-Invariant Autocovariance: Cov(Y_t, Y_(t+k)) depends solely on temporal lag k, not on absolute time t.

Q: 3. What is White Noise and what are its key statistical properties?
A:
White Noise is a purely random, uncorrelated process representing irreducible residual noise:
- Zero Mean: E[ε_t] = 0 for all t.
- Constant Variance: Var(ε_t) = σ²_ε for all t.
- Zero Autocorrelation: Cov(ε_t, ε_s) = 0 for all t ≠ s (variables at different times are completely uncorrelated).
- Essential Diagnostic: In an optimal forecasting model, all model residuals must behave as white noise.

Q: 4. How are Non-Stationary time series transformed into Stationary series?
A:
Non-stationary series are stabilized using mathematical transformations:
- Differencing: First-order differencing (ΔY_t = Y_t - Y_(t-1)) removes linear trends; seasonal differencing removes seasonal patterns.
- Log / Power Transforms: Box-Cox or natural logarithm (log(Y_t)) stabilizes non-constant (heteroscedastic) variance.
- De-trending: Fitting and subtracting a mathematical polynomial trend line.

[Topic: Forecasting Accuracy Metrics & ARIMA Overview]

Q: 1. Define and compare MAE (Mean Absolute Error) and MSE (Mean Squared Error).
A:
Both metrics evaluate error magnitude (e_t = Y_t - F_t) across N test forecasts:
- MAE = (1/N) * Σ |Y_t - F_t|: Measures average absolute magnitude of errors in original physical units; robust to extreme outliers.
- MSE = (1/N) * Σ (Y_t - F_t)²: Squares individual errors; heavily penalizes rare, large errors, making it sensitive to severe forecasting mistakes.

Q: 2. What is RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) and how does it compare to MAE?
A:
RMSE is the square root of Mean Squared Error: RMSE = sqrt((1/N) * Σ (Y_t - F_t)²):
- Interpretable Units: Square root returns the metric to the original physical scale of the observations (unlike MSE).
- Quadratic Penalty: Because errors are squared before averaging, RMSE is always ≥ MAE; the gap between RMSE and MAE highlights the presence and frequency of large outlier errors.

Q: 3. Provide a conceptual overview of the Box-Jenkins ARIMA Modeling Framework.
A:
The Box-Jenkins methodology is an iterative 4-stage framework for building AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average models:
- 1. Identification: Testing stationarity (ADF test) and examining ACF/PACF plots to choose orders p, d, q.
- 2. Estimation: Estimating model coefficients using maximum likelihood estimation (MLE).
- 3. Diagnostic Checking: Verifying that model residuals conform to white noise (Ljung-Box test).
- 4. Forecasting: Generating probabilistic confidence intervals for future horizons.

Q: 4. What do the parameters (p, d, q) represent in an ARIMA(p, d, q) model?
A:
ARIMA models integrate three distinct modeling components:
- p (AutoRegressive Order - AR): Number of lagged historical observations used as linear predictors (Y_(t-1), ..., Y_(t-p)).
- d (Degree of Differencing - I): Number of consecutive differencing steps required to achieve stationarity.
- q (Moving Average Order - MA): Number of lagged forecast error terms (ε_(t-1), ..., ε_(t-q)) incorporated into the prediction.

[Topic: Introduction to Time Series]

Q: 1. Time Series kya hai aur Computer Science aur Business me iski kya importance hai?
A:
Time series sequential data observations ka ek set hota hai jo uniform, continuous time intervals (hourly, daily, monthly) par record hota hai:
- Computer Science: Server workload, CPU/RAM utilization, network traffic patterns monitor karne aur cybersecurity anomalies detect karne me use hota hai.
- Business & Finance: Stock market prices, monthly sales trends, inventory requirements, aur economic indicators track karta hai.
- Core Purpose: Past historical patterns analyze karke future trends aur values forecast karna.

Q: 2. Time Series Analysis ke primary objectives aur applications kya hain?
A:
Time series analysis ke chaar main objectives hote hain:
- Description: Data me mojood structural patterns (trends, seasonal cycles) ko identify aur isolate karna.
- Explanation: Samajhna ki external factors variable ko time ke saath kaise impact kar rahe hain.
- Forecasting: Resource scheduling aur capacity planning ke liye future values predict karna.
- Control: Automated systems me threshold alerts set karna (jaise traffic spike aane par cloud servers auto-scale karna).

Q: 3. Continuous aur Discrete Time Series me kya difference hai?
A:
Observations time continuum me kaise record hoti hain, is basis par classification hoti hai:
- Continuous Time Series: Har ek point of time par continuously record hoti hai (jaise analog ECG machine, continuous seismograph, temperature sensors).
- Discrete Time Series: Fixed discrete time intervals par sample aur record ki jaati hai (jaise daily closing stock price, monthly sales revenue, yearly GDP).

Q: 4. Cross-Sectional, Time Series, aur Panel Data me kya fark hai?
A:
Temporal depth aur subject breadth ke basis par teen formats hote hain:
- Cross-Sectional Data: Multiple entities (jaise 500 companies) ka data ek single fixed point of time par collect karna.
- Time Series Data: Ek single entity ka data multiple successive time intervals par observe karna.
- Panel (Longitudinal) Data: Dono ka hybrid, jahan multiple entities ko multiple time periods me track kiya jata hai.

[Topic: Components of Time Series (Four Forces)]

Q: 1. Time Series ke "Four Forces" (Components) kaunse hain?
A:
Classical time series chaar basic behavioral forces se drive hoti hai:
- Secular Trend (T): Long-term upward, downward, ya steady movement.
- Seasonal Variations (S): Short-term regular periodic fluctuations jo 1 year ke andar repeat hoti hain.
- Cyclical Variations (C): Medium-to-long term wave-like oscillations jo business cycles se related hoti hain.
- Irregular / Random Fluctuations (I): Unpredictable erratic shocks jo sudden random events se aate hain.

Q: 2. Secular Trend (T) kya hai aur iske long-term characteristics kya hote hain?
A:
Secular trend time series ki overall smooth long-term direction ko represent karta hai:
- Sustained Direction: Long period me continuous growth (jaise tech adoption, world population) ya decline (jaise fatal disease death rate) show karta hai.
- Noise Filter: Daily market volatility aur short-term seasonal spikes ko ignore karta hai.
- Baseline Foundation: Yeh foundational baseline banta hai jiske around baaki components oscillate karte hain.

Q: 3. Seasonal Variations (S) aur Cyclical Variations (C) me kya difference hai?
A:
Dono me cycle length aur driving forces ka main difference hota hai:
- Seasonal Variations (S): Strict regular rhythm jo 1 year ke andar complete hoti hai (jaise festivals me retail shopping spike, summer me AC sales); weather ya cultural calendar se driven.
- Cyclical Variations (C): Long-term non-periodic wave oscillations jo 3 se 10+ saal tak chalti hain; economic business cycles (Boom, Recession, Depression, Recovery) se driven.

Q: 4. Irregular ya Random Fluctuations (I) kya hote hain aur inka reason kya hai?
A:
Irregular fluctuations random residual variations hoti hain jo T, S, aur C ke baad bachti hain:
- Sudden Shocks: Unpredictable events jaise natural disasters (earthquake, flood), war, strikes, ya pandemics se trigger hoti hain.
- Non-Deterministic: Inhein mathematically predict ya model nahi kiya ja sakta.
- Short-Lived: Short temporary shock create karti hain, jiske baad system gradually normal baseline par recover ho jata hai.

[Topic: Mathematical Decomposition Models]

Q: 1. Additive Decomposition Model (Y = T + S + C + I) ko explain karein.
A:
Additive model assume karta hai ki observed value chaaro independent components ka linear sum hai:
- Formula: Y_t = T_t + S_t + C_t + I_t.
- Measurement Units: Sabhi components (T, S, C, I) original data units me measure hote hain.
- Core Assumption: Seasonal swings ka absolute magnitude constant rehta hai, chahe overall trend level badhe ya ghate.

Q: 2. Multiplicative Decomposition Model (Y = T * S * C * I) ko explain karein.
A:
Multiplicative model assume karta hai ki components independently nahi, balki proportionally interact karte hain:
- Formula: Y_t = T_t * S_t * C_t * I_t.
- Units: Trend (T) original units me hota hai; Seasonal (S), Cyclical (C), aur Irregular (I) percentages ya relative ratios hote hain.
- Core Assumption: Seasonal fluctuations ka amplitude directly trend level ke proportional hota hai (growth ke saath seasonal peaks bhi badi hoti jaati hain).

Q: 3. Additive Model ke merits aur demerits kya hain?
A:
Additive model calculation me simple hai par practical constraints ke saath aata hai:
- Merits: Intuitive hai, simple subtraction se compute hota hai, aur uniform seasonal swings me accurate rehta hai.
- Demerits: Exponential growth ya percentage compounding cases me fail ho jata hai jahan business size badhne par seasonal variation bhi expand hoti hai.

Q: 4. Multiplicative Model kab choose karna chahiye aur iska Additive se kya relation hai?
A:
Model selection seasonal swing aur trend ke visual relation par depend karta hai:
- Choose Multiplicative: Jab trend badhne ke saath seasonal peaks aur troughs bhi bade hote ja rahe hon (aam business aur web traffic data).
- Choose Additive: Jab seasonal variation ki absolute height saal-dar-saal lagbhag constant rahe.
- Log Transform: Log lene par multiplicative model additive me convert ho jata hai: log(Y) = log(T) + log(S) + log(C) + log(I).

UNIT 2: MEASUREMENT OF TREND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Topic: Non-Mathematical Trend Estimation]

Q: 1. Free-Hand Curve Method kya hai aur iski main limitations kya hain?
A:
Free-hand curve method me data points ko graph par plot karke visual estimate se smooth curve banaya jata hai:
- Procedure: Raw data ko plot karke visual center ke through smooth continuous trend line draw karte hain.
- Advantage: Simple aur fast hai, koi mathematical formulas calculate nahi karne padte.
- Major Limitations: Highly subjective hai; alag-alag analysts alag curve banayenge, jisse objective forecasting possible nahi hoti.

Q: 2. Method of Semi-Averages se linear trend kaise find kiya jata hai?
A:
Method of Semi-Averages poore dataset ko do equal halves me divide karta hai:
- Step 1: Data ko do equal parts me divide karein (jaise 8 saal ke data ko do 4-4 saal ke parts me).
- Step 2: Dono parts ka arithmetic mean (semi-average) calculate karein.
- Step 3: Har semi-average ko uske corresponding time interval ke midpoint par plot karein.
- Step 4: Dono points ko straight line se connect karke secular linear trend line extend karein.

Q: 3. Odd number of years hone par Semi-Averages method kaise apply hota hai?
A:
Jab total years N odd hon (jaise 7 ya 9 saal), toh data directly do equal integers me split nahi ho sakta:
- Omission Method: Bilkul center wale middle year ko calculation se omit (drop) kar dete hain.
- Equal Split: Remaining (N - 1) years ko do perfectly equal halves me divide kiya jata hai.
- Example: Agar 9 saal ka data hai (1 to 9), toh 5th year drop karke 1–4 aur 6–9 ke semi-averages nikaale jaate hain.

Q: 4. Method of Semi-Averages ke merits aur demerits kya hain?
A:
Semi-Averages technique objectivity aur simplicity ka balance provide karti hai:
- Merits: Completely objective hai (sabka exact same answer aayega); complex formulas ke bina easy arithmetic.
- Demerits: Strictly straight line trend assume karta hai; agar actual trend curved, exponential, ya parabolic ho toh fail ho jata hai.

[Topic: Mathematical Trend Fitting (Least Squares)]

Q: 1. Linear Trend (y = a + bx) fit karne ke liye Principle of Least Squares explain karein.
A:
Least Squares method actual values aur trend line values ke beech ke squared differences ko minimize karta hai:
- Equation: y = a + bx, jahan 'a' y-intercept hai aur 'b' constant rate of change (slope) hai.
- Normal Equations:
  1) Σy = Na + bΣx
  2) Σxy = aΣx + bΣx²
- Objective: Σ(y - y_hat) = 0 aur Σ(y - y_hat)² minimum hona ensure karta hai.

Q: 2. Origin shift karke (Σx = 0) normal equations ko kaise simplify kiya jata hai?
A:
Origin x = 0 ko middle year par shift karne se Σx zero ho jata hai, jisse calculations bohot easy ho jaati hain:
- Direct Formulas: a = Σy / N (y ka arithmetic mean) aur b = Σxy / Σx².
- Even Periods: Jab N even ho, toh half-year units use karte hain: x = ..., -3, -1, +1, +3, ... taaki Σx = 0 bana rahe.
- Advantage: Equations ko simultaneously solve karne ki zaroorat nahi padti.

Q: 3. Second-Degree Parabolic Curve (y = a + bx + cx²) fit karne ka procedure kya hai?
A:
Parabolic quadratic curve accelerating ya turning-point trends ko model karta hai:
- Equation: y = a + bx + cx².
- Normal Equations (origin centered at Σx = 0):
  1) Σy = Na + cΣx²
  2) Σxy = bΣx²
  3) Σx²y = aΣx² + cΣx⁴
- Parameter 'b' directly b = Σxy / Σx² se nikalta hai; 'a' aur 'c' equations 1 aur 3 ko simultaneously solve karke nikaale jaate hain.

Q: 4. Exponential Trend Curve (y = ab^x) kaise fit ki jaati hai?
A:
Exponential trend compounding geometric growth (jaise tech adoption, user growth) model karta hai:
- Equation: y = a * b^x.
- Log Transformation: Dono side log lene par linear equation banti hai: log(y) = log(a) + x * log(b).
- Let Y = log(y), A = log(a), aur B = log(b); equation Y = A + Bx ban jati hai.
- Standard least squares se A aur B find karke antilog se original 'a' aur 'b' calculate kiye jaate hain.

[Topic: Moving Average Method]

Q: 1. Moving Average Method kya hai aur yeh fluctuations ko kaise smooth karta hai?
A:
Moving Average method fixed time window 'm' ke successive overlapping averages calculate karta hai:
- Smoothing Effect: Short-term seasonal aur irregular noise ko average out karke underlying secular trend expose karta hai.
- Cycle Elimination: Agar window 'm' exact cycle length ke barabar ho, toh wo specific periodic cycle completely neutralize ho jaata hai.
- Non-Parametric: Linear ya quadratic ka rigid assumption nahi rakhta, local data ke according bend ho jata hai.

Q: 2. Odd-Period Moving Averages (3-yearly aur 5-yearly) kaise calculate hote hain?
A:
Odd period 'm' hone par average exact middle calendar year ke saamne align hota hai:
- 3-Yearly Moving Average: y1, y2, y3 ka average year 2 ke saamne; y2, y3, y4 ka average year 3 ke saamne likhte hain.
- 5-Yearly Moving Average: y1 se y5 ka average year 3 ke saamne; y2 se y6 ka average year 4 ke saamne likhte hain.
- Boundary Loss: 3-yearly me start aur end ka 1-1 saal loss hota hai; 5-yearly me start aur end ke 2-2 saal loss hote hain.

Q: 3. Even-Period Moving Averages (4-yearly centered) me Centering kyun zaroori hoti hai?
A:
Even period 'm' me average do calendar years ke beech me fall karta hai:
- Step 1: 4-yearly moving averages calculate karein; yeh do saalon ke midpoint (jaise year 2 aur 3 ke beech) me aate hain.
- Step 2: Do consecutive 4-yearly averages ka mean (centering) nikaalein; yeh centered value exact calendar year (year 3) ke aage align ho jaati hai.
- Temporal alignment maintain karke original data ke saath direct comparison possible karta hai.

Q: 4. Moving Average Method ke merits aur demerits kya hain?
A:
Moving averages simple aur effective local smoothing deta hai par forecasting me limit hota hai:
- Merits: Highly flexible hai, compute karna easy hai, cycle length pata hone par oscillations smoothly cancel karta hai.
- Demerits: Start aur end periods ke data points lost ho jaate hain; future periods ke liye mathematical forecast formula provide nahi karta.

UNIT 3: SEASONAL VARIATIONS & GROWTH CURVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Topic: Measurement of Seasonal Indices]

Q: 1. Seasonal Index kya hota hai aur yeh kya measure karta hai?
A:
Seasonal Index ek normalized percentage ratio hai jo kisi season ki relative intensity measure karta hai:
- Baseline 100%: Poore saal ke average ko 100% baseline set kiya jata hai.
- Interpretation: Agar Q4 ka seasonal index 130% hai, iska matlab Q4 ki sales annual quarterly average se 30% higher rehti hai.
- Normalized Sum: Quarterly data me chaaro indices ka sum 400 hona chahiye; monthly data me 1200 hona chahiye.

Q: 2. Method of Simple Averages se Seasonal Indices kaise nikaale jaate hain?
A:
Seasonal index calculate karne ka sabse basic method:
- Step 1: Data ko table me arrange karein jahan rows me years aur columns me quarters/months hon.
- Step 2: Har quarter column ka average across all years (Quarterly Average x_bar) calculate karein.
- Step 3: Sabhi quarterly averages ka Grand Average G_bar nikaalein.
- Step 4: Seasonal Index = (x_bar / G_bar) * 100 (aur total 400 par adjust karein).
- Assumption: Tabhi valid hai jab koi long-term trend na ho.

Q: 3. Ratio-to-Trend Method seasonal variations kaise isolate karta hai?
A:
Ratio-to-Trend method underlying secular trend ko account karke seasonal index nikaalta hai:
- Step 1: Least squares method se linear trend line fit karein aur har period ke liye trend value T calculate karein.
- Step 2: Actual value Y ko corresponding trend value T se divide karein: (Y / T) * 100.
- Step 3: Har season ke ratios ka median ya mean lekar irregular variations ko eliminate karein.
- Step 4: Final indices ko 400 (quarterly) ya 1200 (monthly) total ke according normalize karein.

Q: 4. Ratio-to-Moving Average Method sabse reliable kyun mana jaata hai?
A:
Ratio-to-Moving Average trend aur cyclical dono components ko eliminate karta hai:
- Step 1: Centered 12-month (ya 4-quarter) moving average calculate karein; yeh (T * C) ko capture karta hai.
- Step 2: Original value Y ko moving average se divide karein: Y / (T * C) = (T * S * C * I) / (T * C) = S * I.
- Step 3: Different years ke S * I ratios ka average lekar random noise (I) cancel karein, jisse pure Seasonal Index (S) bachta hai.
- Global Standard: Most robust decomposition technique mani jaati hai.

[Topic: Deseasonalization and Link Relatives Method]

Q: 1. Deseasonalization kya hai aur time series ko seasonally adjust kaise kiya jata hai?
A:
Deseasonalization seasonal effects ko remove karke underlying trend aur cyclical movement ko spotlight karta hai:
- Additive Adjustment: Deseasonalized Series = Y - S.
- Multiplicative Adjustment: Deseasonalized Series = (Y / S) * 100.
- Purpose: Successive periods ke true performance ko compare karna bina seasonal festival ya weather spikes ke bias ke.

Q: 2. Pearson ka Method of Link Relatives kaise kaam karta hai?
A:
Link Relatives method har period ko pichhle immediate period ke percentage ke roop me express karta hai:
- Step 1: Link Relative = (Current Period Value / Preceding Period Value) * 100.
- Step 2: Har season ke link relatives ka average (median) calculate karein.
- Step 3: Chain Relatives calculate karein jahan period 1 ko 100 rakha jata hai: Chain Relative = (Link Relative * Preceding Chain Relative) / 100.
- Step 4: End period ke trend discrepancy ko correct karke final seasonal indices normalize karein.

Q: 3. Seasonal variations measure karne ke chaar methods ko compare karein.
A:
Har method simplicity aur accuracy me different trade-off rakhta hai:
- Simple Averages: Sabse easy, par tabhi accurate jab trend zero ya negligible ho.
- Ratio-to-Trend: Effective jab trend mathematical equation (linear/parabolic) follow karta ho.
- Ratio-to-Moving Average: Sabse powerful aur flexible; bina mathematical line assume kiye trend aur cycle filter karta hai.
- Link Relatives: Thorough method hai par calculations thodi complex aur extensive hoti hain.

Q: 4. Business aur Economy me seasonally adjusted time series ka kya role hai?
A:
Seasonally adjusted figures true operational health evaluate karne me critical hote hain:
- Real Growth vs Calendar Spike: Samajhna ki sales badhna real business expansion hai ya normal festival seasonal peak.
- Policy Decisions: Government agencies inflation aur employment data ko seasonally adjust karke policy interest rates decide karti hain.
- Forecasting: Future baseline trend forecast karke use seasonal index se multiply karke realistic targets banaye jaate hain.

[Topic: Growth Curves in IT and Technology Adoption]

Q: 1. Growth Curves kya hain aur IT / Tech Adoption me inka kya role hai?
A:
Growth curves non-linear models hote hain jo saturation ceiling ki taraf badhti growth trajectory ko represent karte hain:
- Asymptotic Ceiling: Linear trend ki tarah tech products forever grow nahi kar sakte; market capacity limit (saturation) hoti hai.
- IT Applications: Internet users growth, smartphone penetration, cloud adoption, software licensing diffusion.
- S-Shape Pattern: Slow start (incubation), tezi se exponential growth, aur finally market saturation par plateau.

Q: 2. Modified Exponential Growth Curve (y = k + ab^x) explain karein.
A:
Modified Exponential curve me growth rate upper asymptote ki taraf exponentially slow hoti hai:
- Formula: y = k + a * b^x, jahan k upper saturation limit hai, 'a' negative hota hai, aur 0 < b < 1.
- Property: Saturation ceiling k aur actual value y ke beech ka gap har period constant percentage 'b' se reduce hota jata hai.
- Application: Un tech products ke liye jo market launch hote hi fast adoption lete hain aur phir gradually stabilize hote hain.

Q: 3. Gompertz Growth Curve (y = k * a^(b^x)) kya hai aur IT me kahan use hoti hai?
A:
Gompertz curve ek asymmetrical S-shaped curve hai jisme percentage growth constant rate se decline karti hai:
- Formula: y = k * a^(b^x), jahan k saturation limit hai, 0 < a < 1, aur 0 < b < 1.
- Asymmetry: Inflection point jaldi aa jata hai (y = k / e ≈ 37% of saturation par), jisse growth logistic curve se pehle slow hona shuru hoti hai.
- IT Use: Mobile subscriber count, commercial software replacement, aur enterprise SaaS customer growth predict karne me.

Q: 4. Logistic Growth Curve (S-Curve) aur iske teen adoption phases explain karein.
A:
Logistic Curve perfectly symmetrical S-shaped diffusion curve provide karta hai jo technology adoption ka standard hai:
- Formula: y = k / (1 + b * e^(-ax)), jahan k market carrying capacity / ceiling hai.
- Symmetry: Inflection point exact midpoint y = k / 2 par hota hai jahan growth velocity maximum hoti hai.
- Three Phases: 1) Slow initial incubation (Innovators), 2) Rapid exponential growth (Early/Late Majority), 3) Saturation plateau (Laggards).

UNIT 4: INTRODUCTION TO FORECASTING & ERRORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Topic: Smoothing Techniques & Exponential Smoothing]

Q: 1. Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES) kya hai aur moving averages se better kyun hai?
A:
SES ek weighted average method hai jo purane observations ko exponentially declining weights assign karta hai:
- Formula: F_(t+1) = α * Y_t + (1 - α) * F_t, jahan α smoothing constant hai (0 ≤ α ≤ 1).
- Recency Weighting: Recent data ko maximum weight milta hai; purana data exponentially decay hota hai.
- Storage Efficient: Long historical window store karne ki jagah sirf current forecast aur latest actual value store karni hoti hai.

Q: 2. Smoothing parameter α forecast responsiveness aur stability ko kaise affect karta hai?
A:
Smoothing parameter α responsiveness aur noise filtering ke beech balance control karta hai:
- High α (close to 1): Recent observation ko heavy weight deta hai; rapid changes ko quickly catch karta hai par noise se easily disturb hota hai.
- Low α (close to 0): Historical smoothed forecast ko heavy weight deta hai; stable rehta hai par actual shifts ko catch karne me lag karta hai.
- Standard Practice: Error metrics (MSE) minimize karke α usually 0.1 se 0.3 ke beech select kiya jata hai.

Q: 3. Holt's Linear Exponential Smoothing method trend ko kaise handle karta hai?
A:
Holt's two-parameter smoothing SES ko extend karke secular linear trends ko incorporate karta hai:
- Level Equation: L_t = α * Y_t + (1 - α) * (L_(t-1) + T_(t-1)), baseline level update karta hai.
- Trend Equation: T_t = β * (L_t - L_(t-1)) + (1 - β) * T_(t-1), trend slope ko parameter β se update karta hai.
- Forecast Formula: Future h steps ahead ka forecast F_(t+h) = L_t + h * T_t hota hai.

Q: 4. Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing kya hai?
A:
Holt-Winters teen parameters use karke Level, Trend, aur Seasonality ko ek saath forecast karta hai:
- Three Parameters: α (Level), β (Trend slope), aur γ (Seasonal indices).
- Multiplicative Formula: F_(t+h) = (L_t + h * T_t) * S_(t+h-m).
- Industry Standard: Fast computation aur low memory ke saath accurate multi-period seasonal economic forecasts deta hai.

[Topic: Stationarity and White Noise]

Q: 1. Stationary Time Series kya hai aur forecasting me iski kya importance hai?
A:
Stationary time series wo hoti hai jiske statistical properties time par depend nahi karte:
- Constant Structure: Modeling algorithms (jaise ARIMA) assume karte hain ki underlying rules time ke saath badal nahi rahe.
- Predictability: Agar mean aur variance time ke saath constantly change ho rahe hon, toh past data future ke behavior ko predict nahi kar sakta.

Q: 2. Weak (Covariance) Stationarity ko Mean, Variance, aur Autocovariance ke terms me define karein.
A:
Ek time series Y_t weakly stationary hoti hai agar wo teen conditions satisfy kare:
- Constant Mean: Sabhi time t par E[Y_t] = μ constant ho (koi trend na ho).
- Constant Variance: Sabhi time t par Var(Y_t) = σ² constant ho (homoscedasticity).
- Time-Invariant Autocovariance: Cov(Y_t, Y_(t+k)) sirf lag k par depend kare, absolute time t par nahi.

Q: 3. White Noise kya hai aur iski key properties kya hain?
A:
White Noise ek purely random aur uncorrelated stochastic process hai jo residual error represent karta hai:
- Zero Mean: Har time t par E[ε_t] = 0.
- Constant Variance: Har time t par Var(ε_t) = σ²_ε.
- Zero Autocorrelation: Kisi bhi time t ≠ s ke liye Cov(ε_t, ε_s) = 0.
- Ideal Model Residual: Perfect forecasting model ka residual hamesha white noise hona chahiye.

Q: 4. Non-Stationary time series ko Stationary kaise banaya jata hai?
A:
Non-stationary series ko stable karne ke liye mathematical transformations lagate hain:
- Differencing: First-order differencing (ΔY_t = Y_t - Y_(t-1)) linear trend remove karti hai; seasonal differencing seasonality remove karti hai.
- Log Transforms: Natural logarithm (log(Y_t)) non-constant variance (heteroscedasticity) ko stabilize karta hai.
- De-trending: Trend line fit karke actual data se subtract kar dena.

[Topic: Forecasting Accuracy Metrics & ARIMA Overview]

Q: 1. MAE (Mean Absolute Error) aur MSE (Mean Squared Error) ko compare karein.
A:
Dono metrics forecast error (e_t = Y_t - F_t) ka magnitude measure karte hain:
- MAE = (1/N) * Σ |Y_t - F_t|: Absolute errors ka mean; original units me hota hai aur extreme outliers se easily distort nahi hota.
- MSE = (1/N) * Σ (Y_t - F_t)²: Errors ko square karta hai; bade errors ko heavily penalize karta hai, isliye sensitive to outliers hota hai.

Q: 2. RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) kya hai aur yeh MAE se kaise different hai?
A:
RMSE Mean Squared Error ka square root hota hai: RMSE = sqrt((1/N) * Σ (Y_t - F_t)²):
- Original Scale: Square root lene se unit MSE ki tarah squared nahi rehti, original data units me aati hai.
- Outlier Sensitivity: Squaring ki wajah se RMSE hamesha ≥ MAE hota hai; RMSE aur MAE ka gap jitna bada ho, model me utne zyada extreme outlier errors hote hain.

Q: 3. Box-Jenkins ARIMA Modeling Framework ka conceptual overview dein.
A:
Box-Jenkins methodology AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average models build karne ka 4-stage process hai:
- 1. Identification: Stationarity check karna (ADF test) aur ACF/PACF plots se orders p, d, q determine karna.
- 2. Estimation: Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) se model coefficients calculate karna.
- 3. Diagnostic Checking: Model residuals ka check karna ki wo white noise hain ya nahi (Ljung-Box test).
- 4. Forecasting: Validated model se future periods ke confidence intervals ke saath forecasts generate karna.

Q: 4. ARIMA(p, d, q) model me parameters (p, d, q) kya represent karte hain?
A:
ARIMA model teen components ka combination hota hai:
- p (AutoRegressive - AR): Past lagged observations (Y_(t-1), ..., Y_(t-p)) ki number jo current value predict karne me use hoti hain.
- d (Integrated - I): Stationarity achieve karne ke liye kitni baar differencing ki gayi.
- q (Moving Average - MA): Past forecast error terms (ε_(t-1), ..., ε_(t-q)) ki number jo model me include hoti hain.
